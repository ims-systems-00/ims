Trust and governance

Why a stranger can rely on it.

A rating is only credible if it can be trusted without seeing the records. Everything about Relyable is built for that.

Evidence, not uploads

Verification tier is set by the connector that read the evidence, never by the business. Every item is hashed with its source and time, chained and anchored externally, so a rating can be audited back to its records without exposing them.

Published, versioned model

Domains, weights, decay, caps and sector profiles are public. Changes are announced and versioned; history is re-rated so trends stay comparable. The model version is stamped on every report.

Independent Rating Council

The model is owned by a council with independent health & safety, insurance, audit and legal members. It calibrates the model quarterly against real outcomes shared by insurers and publishes an annual validation report.

Externally assured

The rating engine, ledger and controls are reviewed under an ISAE 3000 / SOC 2-style assurance programme. Reports are signed; verification is free and public.

Right to dispute

A business can dispute any item. Disputed items are excluded while under review and shown as such. Businesses see who has read their report.

Data minimised

Readers receive the rating, band, tier, verified ratio, domain scores and sourced flags, never the underlying records or personal data. Relyable is operated by iMS Tech Global under the Relyable Standard; iMS OS customers are rated by the same rules and caps as everyone else.

Questions

I didn't ask for a rating.

You didn't ask for a credit file either. The rating exists because the public record exists; we show you exactly what it's built from, and claiming is free.

My rating is low, that's unfair.

A Tier 0 rating is absence of proof, not proof of absence. It's capped and labelled unverified so nobody mistakes it for a judgement. Connect your HR system and your certificates and you'll see the real number.

Why let my insurer see it?

They can already see the public-record version. What you control is whether they see the verified one, and the verified one is what earns better terms.

Do readers see my records?

No. Readers get the rating, band, tier, verified ratio, domain scores and sourced flags. Never documents, never people's names.

Do I need iMS OS?

No. Relyable accepts evidence from any supported system. iMS OS is one way to raise and hold a rating, the one where it's built in.

For businesses

How to get your Relyable rating

Every UK business already has a Tier 0 rating from public records. Here is how to claim it, raise it, and decide what the world sees. It is free. You can connect systems, submit evidence manually, or both.

1 · Find and claim

Prove you act for the business

Search your name or company number, open the report and choose Claim. Confirm with a Companies House officer match or an email at your registered domain. A director approves the first claim.

2 · Confirm your scope

Only what applies to you is rated

We pre-fill your scope from Companies House: sector, size, and what you do (client sites, vehicles, lifting equipment, food, regulated activity, personal data). Confirm or correct it. Anything out of scope is marked not applicable and drops out of the denominator, so a consultancy is never marked down for having no SSIP. Declarations are cross-checked against the public record.

3 · Add evidence

Connect, or submit manually

Connect the systems you already use (Tier 3), or submit certificates, policies and insurance documents by hand. Submitted documents are checked with the issuer where possible (Tier 2) and otherwise count as self-declared (Tier 1).

4 · Decide what is visible

You control the detail

Your rating, band, tier and domain overview are public, like a credit band. Your policies and records are never shown. You choose whether to list your certifications on the public report.

Submit evidence manually

For businesses that cannot connect a system yet, or for one-off items. Each submission is reviewed; items with an issuer reference are verified with the issuer and count as Tier 2.

Verified with the issuer within 2 working days where a reference is given.

What is public, and what is not

  • Public: your rating, band, tier, verified ratio, the domain overview (eight scores), and sourced public-record flags.
  • Never public: your policies, procedures, records, documents, people's names, incident details, or anything you submit or connect.
  • Your choice: whether to list certifications (name, issuer, validity) on the public report. Tick the box per item, or manage them under Visibility.
  • Shared on request only: the full report with explanation, for readers you approve or who hold a report id you issued.

Visibility settings

Decide once; change any time. Owner only; asks for your passkey again.

Not on Companies House?

Sole traders, partnerships and charities can request a rating with a UTR, charity number or VAT number. Public-record coverage is thinner, so verification matters more.

Disclaimer

By claiming a rating you confirm you are authorised to act for the business. Your rating, band, tier and domain overview are published and may be read by insurers, buyers, lenders, investors and the public, in the same way as a company's filing history. Policies, procedures, records and documents you connect or submit are never published and are used only to compute the rating and to verify evidence with its issuer. Certifications are listed publicly only where you choose to list them. You can dispute any item, see who has read your full report, and withdraw sharing at any time; the Tier 0 public-record rating remains, as with a credit file. Ratings are statements of verified evidence at the time shown and are not a guarantee of safety, legality or solvency.

Relyable Standard v1.1

What a rating means, the same for every reader.

The Standard fixes the scale, the bands, the verification tiers, the evidence each domain requires and the caps. It is published, versioned and governed by the Rating Council.

Bands

AAssured 850–1000Verified (Tier 2–3), every mandatory item current, no caps. Rely on it.
BStrong 700–849Mandatory items current; minor gaps; verified ratio ≥ 50%. Rely with a condition on the open items.
CDeveloping 550–699A mandatory gap, thin verification or mostly self-declared. Ask for verification.
DAt risk 400–549Multiple gaps or an adverse public event. Conditions; verification will show if the record is current.
EUnverified 0–399Little or no verified evidence. Absence of proof, not proof of absence.

Caps, always shown on the report

  • Expired or missing mandatory evidence for the sector profile → ≤ 699
  • Open enforcement notice, prosecution within 5 years, disqualified director → ≤ 549
  • Verified-evidence ratio below 50% → ≤ 749
  • RIDDOR specified injury, investigation open → Safety domain ≤ 40 for 90 days
  • Accounts overdue, insolvency event, strike-off action → ≤ 399

Domains and weights

Certification and accreditation20%
People and competence15%
Safety performance15%
Assets and inspections15%
Policies and governance10%
Supply chain10%
Financial and insurance10%
Information security5%

Domain score = coverage × validity × verification. Rating = Σ weight × domain × 10. Periodic evidence decays on its own clock. Sector profiles set which types are mandatory. Reader lenses re-weight the same evidence for insurance, procurement and lending.

Scope profiles: what applies, and what does not

Not every business needs SSIP, LOLER inspections or a fleet policy. Each organisation has a scope profile, pre-filled from Companies House and confirmed on claim: sector family, size band, and nine attribute flags (employs staff, works on client sites, occupies premises, contractor work, operates vehicles, lifting or pressure equipment, handles food, regulated activity, personal data at scale). Every evidence type and every risk indicator carries a published applicability rule against those flags, with four states.

Required

Counts in full; missing or expired triggers the mandatory cap.

Expected

Counts in full; missing lowers the domain but never caps.

Optional

Adds points if present; never subtracts. Shown grey as "not expected".

Not applicable

Removed from the denominator; its weight is redistributed pro rata. Shown grey with the reason.

  • Renormalisation: domain score = Σ applicable item points ÷ Σ applicable item weights. If a whole domain is out of scope its weight is spread across the others, so every rating is out of 1,000 and comparable across sectors.
  • Core items never switch off: Companies House status and filings, director disqualifications, insolvency notices, HSE enforcement, and employers' liability insurance where staff are employed.
  • Declarations are Tier 1 until corroborated and are cross-checked: an O-licence, a vehicle-related SIC code or a vehicle RIDDOR entry turns "operates vehicles" back on and raises a flag. A not-applicable rate well above the sector norm triggers review.
  • Visible to readers: the scope profile and every not-applicable item appear on the public report, so an insurer can judge the declarations, and "no SSIP" is read as irrelevant for an accountancy firm rather than as a gap.
  • Examples: SSIP is Required for contractor and client-site work, otherwise Not applicable. LOLER is Required where lifting equipment is declared or evidenced. Fire risk assessment is Required for anyone occupying premises. ISO certification is Expected for contracting, food and regulated sectors, Optional elsewhere.

What a rating is, and what it is not

A Relyable rating is an auditable statement of a business's verified evidence of how it runs, at the time shown. It is not a guarantee of safety, legality or solvency, a prediction of loss, or a substitute for a survey where one is legally required. Readers must read band and tier together and must not treat Tier 0/1 ratings as verified.
